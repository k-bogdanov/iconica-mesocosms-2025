# 70 years of P fertilisation is associated with differences in microbial community structure and N cycling under contrasting short-term C inputs

**Abstract for the study**
>In grassland agroecosystems, phosphorus (P) fertilisation is essential for productivity, but its long-term role in regulating soil carbon (C) and nitrogen (N) transformations remains uncertain. Soils from a 70-year field fertilisation experiment at the Winchmore Research Station, New Zealand, were used to examine how single superphosphate (SSP) affected short-term C and N cycling and soil microbial communities. Two mesocosm incubations used soils from non-fertilised and fertilised plots (188 or 250 kg ha<sup>-1</sup> yr<sup>-1</sup> SSP), amended with ammonium nitrate (NH<sub>4</sub>NO<sub>3</sub>) and low (ryegrass roots) or high (maize leaves) organic C inputs, representing contrasting residue scenarios under long-term P history. Microbial biomass C (MBC) but not hot-water extractable C were different between SSP treatments in the low-C scenario, with fertilised soil showing lower MBC accumulation than non-fertilised soil. In the maize-amended experiment, N2O-N flux differed between SSP treatments overall, with a significant difference on day 2. Cumulative carbon dioxide (CO<sub>2</sub>) and N<sub>2</sub>O emissions were also higher in the 250 SSP soil. The composite soils differed between SSP treatments in selected inorganic N variables and microbial community structure. Long-term P fertilisation was associated with a consistent decline in *Bradyrhizobium elkanii* and divergent responses among *Mortierella* species across both experiments. Responses of C and N pools and gas emissions differed between the amendment scenarios, but the contributions of residue properties, nutrient inputs, sampling season, and P treatment cannot be separated by the experimental design.
